Easy hiking trails near Iowa City for beginners

If you have never hiked, the hardest part is picking a place to start. These five trails are gentle, easy to follow, and close to town. We compare them below on the things that decide whether a first hike goes well: the surface under your feet, how hilly it is, how long it takes, and where you leave the car.

How to choose the right one

If you want zero surprises

Pick Terry Trueblood or the Iowa River Corridor Trail. Both are largely paved and flat, so there is no mud, no confusing junctions, and no need for special shoes. They are also the best choices if you are pushing a stroller or walking with someone who tires quickly.

If you want to feel like you are really hiking

Try Hickory Hill Park on the wider paths. You get trees, birdsong, and dirt underfoot, with only modest hills. Stay on the main routes for your first visit and treat the narrower side trails as a later challenge.

If you have a car and a free afternoon

Kent Park and Lake Macbride State Park are worth the short drive. The lake views make an easy walk feel like an outing, and the shorter routes are forgiving for new hikers.

What to expect on any of these trails

  • Mud: Dirt paths can stay slick for a day or two after rain. If you want to stay clean, choose a paved trail the day after a storm.
  • Shade: Wooded paths are cool in summer. Open lakeside and river paths can be hot, so wear a hat.
  • Other people: Paved trails are shared with cyclists and runners. Keep to the right and listen for bells.
  • Bugs and ticks: Late spring through summer, wear long socks if you walk through grass and check yourself afterward.

What to bring on an easy hike

  • Water, about half a liter per person for each hour you plan to be out.
  • Comfortable closed shoes with a bit of grip. Running shoes are fine on these trails.
  • A snack, such as a granola bar or fruit.
  • A phone with a charged battery, and a download of the trail map in case your signal drops.
  • Sunscreen, and a light layer if the weather could turn.

Check before you go

Parks close paths for flooding, maintenance, and events, and conditions change from week to week. Check with the park or trail manager for current closures and rules before you leave.
